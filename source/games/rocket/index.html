    How Daily Rocket works

    Every day there is one contract, one world, one shed of parts, and everyone on Earth gets the same ones. Build a rocket, fly it to the pad, land it softly, and make money.

    • The hangar: tap a part in the shed, then tap a glowing slot (or drag it there). Engines go at the bottom of a stage; decouplers split stages; fins, legs, chutes, thrusters and boosters mount in pairs on the side. Watch Δv, thrust-to-weight, and the centre of mass (yellow) against the centre of pressure (blue): pressure below mass is stable in air.
    • Flying: A/D or the arrow buttons turn it (let go and it holds its angle); W/S or the slider set the throttle, Z full, X cut; Space stages; E chutes (again to cut them); F holds retrograde, which is the landing-burn attitude. R restarts, M mutes.
    • Landing: legs take 6 m/s down and 2.5 across, level-ish. On the bare engine bell it's 2 m/s. Down safe off the pad? Relight and hop over.
    • The score is profit: the fee, minus every part you fly and every kilo you burn, plus half the hardware back for anything that lands intact (stages under a chute count), plus bonuses for precision, a soft touchdown and beating the schedule.
    • The twist changes the rules for the day: fuel prices, budgets, the hangar ceiling, missing parts. It moves where the best answer is.
    • Your first flight is your blind run and it stays on the record. Beat the Flight Director's par for three stars, beat it well for a diamond.
